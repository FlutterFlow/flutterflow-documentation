# Navigating External Licenses

Before including third-party code, images, fonts, designs, audio, video, or templates in a FlutterFlow Marketplace item, confirm that the applicable license expressly permits your intended distribution. A license that permits use in a finished app may still prohibit redistributing source assets or offering them inside another template or marketplace.

:::warning[Terms change]
This guide is general information, not legal advice. Do not rely on copied excerpts or an AI-generated license summary. Read the current license for the exact asset and plan you obtained, retain evidence of the terms that applied when you acquired it, and obtain legal advice when the rights are unclear.
:::

## Questions to answer for every dependency

1. **Who owns it?** Identify the original author or rights holder, not only the site where you found the asset.
2. **Which license applies?** Save the license name, version, URL, acquisition date, receipt, and any written permission.
3. **Does it allow commercial use?** Free access does not necessarily grant commercial rights.
4. **Does it allow redistribution in editable form?** Marketplace buyers receive material they can inspect and modify. Permission to ship a compiled app may be insufficient.
5. **Does it allow templates or competing products?** Many design and stock-asset licenses prohibit resale as stock, source files, templates, or products that compete with the original.
6. **What attribution, notice, source, or share-alike obligations apply?** Include all required notices and ensure downstream users can comply.
7. **Do dependencies have separate terms?** A package, font, image set, or design system can contain material under several licenses.
8. **Can you prove the permission?** If permission is custom, obtain it in writing from someone authorized to grant it.

## Common license categories

- **Permissive open-source licenses** such as MIT, BSD, and Apache 2.0 commonly permit commercial redistribution, but still have notice, attribution, patent, or license-text requirements.
- **Copyleft licenses** can require source availability or licensing of derivative work under specified terms. Review the exact license and how your item combines with the code.
- **Stock, design, font, and marketplace licenses** often distinguish a finished end product from a reusable template or source asset. Do not assume an “extended” or “commercial” license permits redistribution in a FlutterFlow item.
- **No license or unclear provenance** means you do not have documented permission to redistribute the material.

## Check the original provider

Provider terms can change and can differ by asset or subscription. Review the current primary terms directly—for example, [UI8 licensing](https://ui8.net/licensing), [Envato Elements license terms](https://elements.envato.com/license-terms), [Creative Market license terms](https://creativemarket.com/licenses/terms/general), and [Canva's Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/). These links are examples, not a determination that a particular asset is allowed or prohibited.

## Obtain explicit permission

If standard terms do not clearly authorize Marketplace redistribution, ask the rights holder for written permission that identifies the asset, your FlutterFlow Marketplace item, whether the item is free or paid, the editable/source format buyers receive, and any limits or attribution requirements. Keep that permission with your submission records.

Your submission must also comply with the current [Marketplace Terms of Service](https://flutterflow.io/tos-marketplace), [Legal Guidelines for Creators](legal-guidelines-for-creators.md), and [Submission Criteria](submission-criteria.md).
